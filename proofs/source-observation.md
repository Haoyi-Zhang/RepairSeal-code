# Natural-source observations and finite scalar correspondence

The two Codeflaws pairs are purposively selected case studies: 4-A-bug-18258406-18258457 and 84-A-bug-9385928-9385933. The supplied versions are existing repairs, not repairs generated by this study. Complete-program inspection supports the following manual correspondence to the project-authored models in `tests/codeflaws_cases.py`; this is not a general C frontend or a random-sample repair-effectiveness estimate.

## Observation and integer semantics

Each original translation unit reads one signed integer from standard input and emits one label or decimal number on standard output. For a case, let `P` be either its buggy or supplied whole program, `K` its corresponding unsigned scalar model, and `encode(x)` the ASCII decimal representation of `x` followed by a newline. The correspondence is

`decode_output(stdout(P(encode(x)))) = K(x)`.

It assumes a successful `scanf` conversion, successful `printf` output and normal completion under standard C semantics. The programs do not check those I/O return values; malformed input, conversion failure and stream failure are outside the claim. The zero returned by `main` is process-success status, not the scalar observation. The decoder strips surrounding whitespace, maps 4A's `YES` to 1 and `NO` to 0, and reads 84A output as a nonnegative ASCII decimal integer. No emitted output approaches its 128-byte limit. Unused arguments and 84A's unread local variable have no observational effect.

The recorded native compiler is Clang 19.1.7 through Zig, targeting `x86_64-unknown-windows-gnu`, with C11 and optimization disabled. Its recorded macro check establishes four-byte `int` and `INT_MAX = 2147483647`; thus signed 32-bit arithmetic is an established property of that recorded target, not an inference from the source spelling `int`. The unsigned kernel explicitly uses 32-bit words.

For 4A, inputs are at most 100 and remainder by 2 is 0 or 1. For 84A, the buggy computation is at most 100000000; the supplied computation divides by 2 before adding, so its largest intermediate is 150000000. The reference model additionally multiplies by 3 before division, reaching at most 300000000. All are nonnegative and below both signed 32-bit `INT_MAX` and `2^32`. Consequently comparison, remainder, division, addition and multiplication agree with their unsigned recodings here: signed division truncation equals nonnegative integer division, divisors are nonzero, and no wraparound or signed overflow occurs. The compiler record supports these type premises; it is not a compiler-correctness proof.

## Domains

The fixed input axes are

- `D4 = {1,...,60} union {97,98,99,100}`;
- `D84 = {2,4,...,120} union {99999994,99999996,99999998,100000000}`.

Each has 64 distinct legal values. Each request has one input axis and hence 64 points; the two cases are separate requests, not a two-axis product. The number 64 is a cardinality, not a word width. Neither axis is the whole legal task domain. The expression identities below hold on the official legal ranges under the same execution assumptions, but each finite certificate and its matched native-domain observations cover only those declared points.

## 4A: positive even parts

The [official Watermelon task](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/4/A) allows integers `1 <= w <= 100` and asks for two positive even parts summing to `w`. Such parts each have weight at least 2, so their sum is even and at least 4. Conversely, for even `w >= 4`, the parts 2 and `w-2` suffice. This derives the reference predicate.

Writing `1[A]` for the indicator of proposition `A`, the decoded buggy observation is `B4(w) = 1[2 divides w]`: its extra comparison is reflexively true after successful input. The supplied observation and reference are

`S4(w) = T4(w) = 1[2 divides w and w >= 4]`.

The model's alternative threshold greater than 2 is equivalent when evenness is required. These formulas also match `value()` and the label decoder. Their only buggy/reference discrepancy on `D4` is at 2. Thus the repair region is exactly `{2}`: the supplied candidate matches the reference there and preserves the buggy observation everywhere else. All three scalar models are defined on `D4`.

## 84A: maximum losses in three turns

The [official Toy Army task](https://codeforces.com/problemset/problem/84/A) allows even `2 <= n <= 100000000`. Two armies of `n` soldiers fire in the order Valera, Arcady, Valera; each surviving shooter selects one target, and multiple shooters may share a target. The following derives the maximum, rather than taking it from expected test outputs.

Let `a` be Arcady's first-turn losses, `b` Valera's second-turn losses, and `c` Arcady's third-turn losses. The second turn has at most `n-a` shooters, so `a+b <= n`. The third turn has `n-b` shooters and `n-a` possible targets, giving

`a+b+c <= a+b+min(n-a,n-b) = n+min(a,b) <= 3n/2`.

This bound is attained: first kill `n/2` distinct Arcady targets; Arcady's `n/2` survivors kill `n/2` Valera targets; Valera's `n/2` survivors then kill Arcady's remaining soldiers. Evenness makes every count integral. Hence the reference value is `3n/2`.

The buggy program takes the integer quotient of 3 by 2 before multiplying, so its decoded observation is `B84(n) = n`. The supplied program adds `n` to its half, giving `S84(n) = n+n/2 = 3n/2`; the normalized reference computes the same integer by multiplication followed by division. These equalities match `value()` on the legal even inputs. All models are defined on `D84`, and the buggy/reference gap is `n/2 >= 1` everywhere. The repair region is therefore all of `D84`. The supplied candidate meets the reference throughout, while outside-region preservation is vacuous, not an additional preservation result.

## Evidence and work boundary

Native traces were not instrumented. The model's canonical trace records its single conditional outcome for 4A and is empty for 84A. These are normalized source-if traces, not measured native paths or short-circuit/machine-branch traces. The receiver binds certificates to the normalized function strings, domains and repair selector. Original translation-unit identity, the manual I/O correspondence and native observations are separate evidence; a scalar certificate alone does not validate a whole C frontend.

The four recorded requests require respectively 1152, 1600, 704 and 896 node-point cells: 4352 in total for full certificate checking and the same 4352 for certificate-free topological evaluation, with matching classifications. This is a negative work result for an acceleration claim. Full evidence also requires production and encoding; the manual source-to-model construction is untimed work, not zero-cost automation. The correspondence establishes neither end-to-end acceleration nor repair-generation success or benchmark-wide effectiveness. Original C text and test payloads remain outside this note.
